# PhilPapers: Category Taxonomy

> The working map of academic philosophy. Its second level is effectively the list of **specializations** a philosopher can have. The top level reproduces the same four-way split that university majors use, plus a fifth area for traditions outside the analytic mainstream.

## The taxonomy (top two levels)
| # | Area | Subfields |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Metaphysics and Epistemology** | Epistemology · Metaphilosophy · Metaphysics · Philosophy of Action · Philosophy of Language · Philosophy of Mind · Philosophy of Religion · M&E, Misc |
| 2 | **Value Theory** | Aesthetics · Applied Ethics · Meta-Ethics · Normative Ethics · Philosophy of Gender, Race, and Sexuality · Philosophy of Law · Social and Political Philosophy · Value Theory, Misc |
| 3 | **Science, Logic, and Mathematics** | Logic and Philosophy of Logic · Philosophy of Biology · Philosophy of Cognitive Science · Philosophy of Computing and Information · Philosophy of Mathematics · Philosophy of Physical Science · Philosophy of Social Science · Philosophy of Probability · General Philosophy of Science · Misc |
| 4 | **History of Western Philosophy** | Ancient Greek and Roman · Medieval and Renaissance · 17th/18th Century · 19th Century · 20th Century · Misc |
| 5 | **Philosophical Traditions** | African/Africana · Asian · Continental · European · Philosophy of the Americas · Misc |
| 6 | **Philosophy, Misc** | |
| 7 | **Other Academic Areas** | |

Below this level, each subfield branches into topics and sub-topics; for example, Philosophy of Mind leads to consciousness, then to specific theories. Many categories carry an editor-written summary with key works and introductions.

## The same partition in university requirements
| PhilPapers area | Harvard concentration | Princeton major | Cambridge Part IA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metaphysics and Epistemology | Area 2: Contemporary Metaphysics and Epistemology | (1) metaphysics | Paper 1 Metaphysics · Paper 3 Meaning |
| Value Theory | Area 3: Contemporary Ethics / Political / Aesthetics | (2) ethics and philosophy of value | Paper 2 Ethics and Political Philosophy |
| Science, Logic, and Mathematics | Area 1: Logic | (3) logic and philosophy of science | Paper 5 Formal Methods |
| History of Western Philosophy | Area 4: History of Philosophy | (4) history of philosophy | Paper 4 Set Texts (Plato, Descartes, Mill) |
| Philosophical Traditions | — (no area requirement) | — | — (some Part II coverage: "Philosophy in the Long Middle Ages," non-Western views of the self in Metaphysics) |

Area 5 is where the institutional curricula are thinnest. African, Asian and Latin American philosophy rarely get their own requirement, which mirrors the canon gaps noted elsewhere in this folder.

## Related: the PhilPapers Surveys (the field's "big questions" list)
- **2009:** the first survey of professional philosophers' views, by Bourget and Chalmers, with **30 questions**. Each question offers a choice between two or more positions (e.g., free will: compatibilism, libertarianism, no free will).
- **2020:** **100 questions** (40 main and 60 additional), answered by nearly **1,800 philosophers**, mostly in North America, Europe and Australasia. The authors caution that the 2020 results cannot be compared directly with 2009 because the surveyed population changed.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **A ready-made skeleton for philosophy specializations.** The second level (about 40 subfields) is the natural list of "specialization paths." The four-area match with Harvard, Princeton and Cambridge shows it is not idiosyncratic.
- **Survey questions are natural "problems."** Each is a crisp question with named rival positions and real distribution data from professionals. That fits a format like "pick a position, defend it, then see what philosophers think."
- **Area 5 is a gap to fill deliberately.** Traditions outside the Western canon are first-class in the taxonomy but absent from most curricula.
