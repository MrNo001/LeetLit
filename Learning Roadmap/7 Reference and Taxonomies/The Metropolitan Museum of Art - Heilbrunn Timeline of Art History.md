# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: *Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History*

> Art history as a **matrix**: world regions crossed with time periods, each cell a short chronology with key works and events, overlaid with a thousand thematic essays. It is the art counterpart of the MLA's language × period structure, and a direct counterweight to single-story surveys like Gombrich.

## Structure
| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Chronologies** (~300) | One region in one time period. Each has up to ten representative works of art, a timeline, an overview and key events. Navigation uses world maps: pick a period, then a region |
| **Essays** (1,000+) | Themes: artistic movements and periods, archaeological sites, empires and civilizations, recurrent themes and concepts, media, and individual artists |
| **Works of art** (~8,000) | Objects from the Met's collection, placed chronologically, geographically and thematically |
| **Keywords** (~3,700) | Tags linking everything: art movement and style · artists and makers · geography (present-day nations and historical regions) · time period · material and technique · object type · subject matter |

- **Growth:** it debuted on 3 October 2000 with the ancient world (10,000 BC – AD 500) and now runs from prehistory to the present.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **A proven tag schema for art:** movement/style, maker, geography, period, material/technique, object type and subject matter. Art nodes in LeetLit could use the same facets, much as the MLA forums suggest facets for literature.
- **Chronology cells are a ready path unit:** "one region × one period, about ten key works, a timeline, an overview." That is close to a NeetCode-style problem set for art.
- **Global by construction:** because the grid covers every region, gaps are visible as empty cells. That makes it a good corrective to Western-only paths (compare Columbia's Art Hum and Gombrich).
