# Bertrand Russell: *A History of Western Philosophy*

> Full title: *…and Its Connection with Political and Social Circumstances from the Earliest Times to the Present Day*. The best-selling one-volume history of philosophy. It grew from Russell's 1941–42 Barnes Foundation lectures and was cited among the works behind his **1950 Nobel Prize in Literature**. It is opinionated, witty, and often unfair.

## The path
| Book | Part | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Ancient Philosophy** | 1. The Pre-Socratics | Milesians → Pythagoras → Heraclitus → Parmenides → Empedocles → Anaxagoras → Atomists → Protagoras |
| | 2. Socrates, Plato and Aristotle | Socrates; Plato's utopia, theory of ideas, immortality, cosmogony; Aristotle's metaphysics, ethics, politics, logic, physics |
| | 3. Ancient Philosophy after Aristotle | Cynics and Sceptics, Epicureans, Stoics, Plotinus |
| **II. Catholic Philosophy** | 1. The Fathers | Judaism and early Christianity; Ambrose, Jerome, Augustine; Benedict; Gregory the Great |
| | 2. The Schoolmen | Eriugena, Anselm, Islamic philosophy, Aquinas, the Franciscans, the decline of scholasticism |
| **III. Modern Philosophy** | 1. From the Renaissance to Hume | Renaissance, Machiavelli, Erasmus and More, Reformation, science, Bacon, Hobbes, Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz, Locke, Berkeley, Hume |
| | 2. From Rousseau to the Present Day | Romanticism, Rousseau, Kant, Hegel, Byron, Schopenhauer, Nietzsche, utilitarians, Marx, Bergson, William James, Dewey, ending with **"The Philosophy of Logical Analysis"** (Russell's own school) |

## Teaching style
- Each philosopher is placed in **social and political circumstances** first, then given an exposition and Russell's often sharp verdict.
- It is the history of philosophy as a story that *culminates in analytic philosophy*. The teleology is part of the book's appeal and its main flaw.

## Reception (why it can't be the only source)
Critics call it full of "overgeneralizations and omissions." A. C. Grayling says it is often inaccurate or unfair, Copleston finds parts misleading, Scruton faults its handling of Kant, and George Steiner calls it "vulgar" and notes it omits Heidegger. It has stayed in print since 1945 anyway.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **"Context → exposition → verdict"** is a durable explainer template. LeetLit should label the *verdict* layer as opinion.
- Its three-book structure (Ancient / Medieval / Modern) is the default macro-periodization of Western philosophy, shared by HoPWaG, the Great Courses, *Sophie's World* and Yale DS.
