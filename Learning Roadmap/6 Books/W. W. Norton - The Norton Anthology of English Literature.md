# W. W. Norton: *The Norton Anthology of English Literature*

> The standard textbook for the English literature survey since 1962. It turns more than a thousand years of writing in English into **six period volumes**, each opening with a long historical introduction and then moving author by author, with headnotes and annotations.

## The path (six period volumes)
| Vol. | Period |
|---|---|
| A | The Middle Ages |
| B | The Sixteenth Century and the Early Seventeenth Century |
| C | The Restoration and the Eighteenth Century |
| D | The Romantic Period |
| E | The Victorian Age |
| F | The Twentieth and Twenty-First Centuries |

The two packages (A–C and D–F) mirror the common two-semester survey split around 1800.

## History
- **1962:** first edition, in two volumes, based on the English survey course M. H. Abrams and David Daiches taught at Cornell.
- **Abrams** was general editor for the first seven editions. **Stephen Greenblatt**, his deputy from the mid-1990s, succeeded him.
- **7th edition:** six volumes sold as two sets of three, alongside a two-volume version and a single-volume *Major Authors* edition.
- **11th edition (July 2024):** two packages of three volumes, a shorter two-volume edition, and an ebook with embedded video and audio. Norton calls it the most significant revision in decades: more than 300 new selections in the full edition (125 in the shorter one), with Greenblatt joined by seven new editors.

## How a volume works
- A period introduction covering history, society, religion and literary culture.
- Then the authors in chronological order, each with a biographical headnote, and the texts with glosses and footnotes.

## The canon debate
- The anthology has been "assailed by some for being too canonical and by others for faddishly expanding the reading list" (Donadio, 2006, as quoted on Wikipedia).
- In 1999 the rival Longman anthology "mounted a challenge to Norton." David Damrosch, of the Longman anthology, wrote that he "could wish that the new edition of the Norton had reflected more independent thought." Norton replied that its new edition was "not (as Longman personnel have charged) simply an attempt to copy Longman."

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Period volumes are the default literature path:** six eras, each a context introduction followed by author units. This is the period-survey organizing principle, the same one behind Oxford English's period papers.
- **Headnote + annotated text + context** makes a strong "problem page" template: who wrote it, when and why, then the text with glosses.
- **Every edition renegotiates the canon** (11 editions in 62 years). LeetLit paths should be versioned, with their inclusion criteria made explicit.
