# Harold Bloom: *The Western Canon*

> A defense of aesthetic value against what Bloom called the **"School of Resentment."** It builds a canon of 26 authors with **Shakespeare at the center** and reads every other writer in relation to him. Its appendix lists became a popular reading checklist, and Bloom later disowned them.

## The path: 26 authors in three ages
Bloom borrows Giambattista Vico's cycle of ages: a Theocratic age, then Aristocratic, Democratic and Chaotic ages.

| Age | Authors |
|---|---|
| **Aristocratic** | Shakespeare · Dante · Chaucer · Cervantes · Montaigne · Molière · Milton · Samuel Johnson · Goethe |
| **Democratic** | Wordsworth · Jane Austen · Walt Whitman · Emily Dickinson · Dickens · George Eliot · Tolstoy · Ibsen |
| **Chaotic** (20th century) | Freud · Proust · Joyce · Virginia Woolf · Kafka · Borges · Neruda · Pessoa · Beckett |

The book opens with "An Elegy for the Canon" and closes with an "Elegiac Conclusion."

## The appendices (the famous lists)
- Four lists of works Bloom then considered canonical, one per age: **Theocratic, Aristocratic, Democratic, Chaotic**. They run "from the earliest scriptures to Tony Kushner's *Angels in America*" (Wikipedia).
- Bloom later disowned them. In a 2008 Vice interview he said: "The list was not my idea" and "I wish I had nothing to do with it." He had written them at his editor's insistence.

## How a chapter works
- One author or a pair, read through one or two central works, with close attention to voice, character and originality.
- Bloom's test for canonical status is **strangeness**: an originality we either cannot absorb, or have absorbed so completely that we no longer notice it.
- The recurring move is comparison with Shakespeare, plus Bloom's earlier theory of the **anxiety of influence** (1973): strong writers define themselves by struggling with their precursors.

## The "School of Resentment"
- Bloom's label for critical schools that gained ground from the 1970s: Marxist criticism, African-American studies, New Historicism, feminist criticism, and post-structuralism (Lacan, Derrida, Foucault).
- He saw them as putting political and social activism ahead of aesthetic value, and argued they might lead to the canon's "eventual demise."
- Critics answered that canon-making is itself value-laden. Manfred Pfister granted Bloom was partly right, but said the school "deserves to be taken seriously."

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **A hub-and-spoke canon:** a central author with influence links radiating outward is a different graph shape from a chronological line. Literature paths could model **influence edges** ("who read whom") explicitly.
- **Canon lists are contested, even by their authors.** Present every list as *one curated path among several*, with the curator and selection criteria visible.
