# Will Durant: *The Story of Philosophy*

> The book that made philosophy a mass-market bestseller. It began as **Little Blue Books** worker-education pamphlets and was assembled into a hardcover in 1926. It teaches philosophy as **biography plus big ideas**, a few giants at a time.

## The path (11 chapters)
1. **Plato** (with Socrates)
2. **Aristotle** (and Greek science)
3. **Francis Bacon**
4. **Spinoza** (with Descartes)
5. **Voltaire** (with Rousseau) and the French Enlightenment
6. **Kant** (with Hegel) and German idealism
7. **Schopenhauer**
8. **Herbert Spencer**
9. **Nietzsche**
10. **Contemporary European philosophers:** Bergson, Croce, Bertrand Russell
11. **Contemporary American philosophers:** Santayana, William James, John Dewey

**Gaps by design:** the chapter list jumps from Aristotle to Bacon, so there are no medieval chapters. Asian philosophy is omitted (Confucius, the Buddha, Shankara), which Durant acknowledged in the 1933 foreword. Hume, Locke and Berkeley get only brief treatment inside other chapters.

## Chapter anatomy
- Life and times, told vividly → the system, built up with quotations → criticism and assessment → bridge to the next thinker. Ideas are presented as answers to the previous thinker.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **The minimal "great figures" path:** 11 stops from Plato to Dewey is a credible "Philosophy Speedrun" for beginners. Compare Magee's 15 and Warburton's 40.
- **Biography as a hook:** the human story keeps beginners reading, but LeetLit should flag the omissions such curated paths make.
