# Encyclopaedia Britannica: *Great Books of the Western World* and the **Syntopicon**

> A 54-volume canon (Homer to Freud) **plus a concept index**, the *Syntopicon*. It has 102 chapters, each tracing one "Great Idea" through every author in the set down to specific passages. In 1952 it was effectively a hand-built knowledge graph of Western thought.

## Structure
- **1952 edition (54 vols):** Vol. 1, Hutchins, *The Great Conversation* (an introduction to liberal education) · Vols. 2–3, the **Syntopicon** · Vols. 4–54, the works in chronological order of author, from Homer (Vol. 4) to Freud (Vol. 54). Spines are color-coded into four groups: **Imaginative Literature · Mathematics and Natural Sciences · History and Social Science · Philosophy and Theology**.
- **1990 edition (60 vols):** Hutchins's volume dropped, six 20th-century volumes added, new translations. Four texts removed: Apollonius's *Conics*, *Tristram Shandy*, *Tom Jones*, and Fourier's *Analytical Theory of Heat*.

## How the Syntopicon works
- Each idea has an introductory essay by Adler, an **outline of topics** (sub-questions), and for each topic, **references to exact passages** across the set (volume and page).
- It was built by teams who reread each work with one subordinate idea in mind, collected the instances, refined the idea structure, and then indexed. It was designed for "random access" to the Great Conversation.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **The Syntopicon is a ready-made "topics" taxonomy** for the humanities (102 ideas → sub-topics → passages). It parallels NeetCode's topic tags, with cross-cutting concepts spanning philosophy, literature and history.
- **Concept-to-passage links** suggest a data model: `Idea → Topic → Passage(work, location)`. A LeetLit "problem" can be a passage tagged with Syntopicon-style ideas, enabling syntopical (cross-author) exercises.
- **A two-axis canon:** works ordered chronologically *and* ideas indexed thematically are the same dual navigation Columbia's CC uses.
