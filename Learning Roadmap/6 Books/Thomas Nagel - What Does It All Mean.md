# Thomas Nagel: *What Does It All Mean?*

> The purest **problems-first** introduction. A leading philosopher (NYU) works through nine questions **without quoting anyone**, so the reader confronts the problems directly. It is one of the most widely recommended first books in philosophy.

## The path (10 chapters)
1. Introduction
2. How Do We Know Anything? (skepticism)
3. Other Minds
4. The Mind–Body Problem
5. The Meaning of Words
6. Free Will
7. Right and Wrong
8. Justice
9. Death
10. The Meaning of Life

## Chapter anatomy
- A question posed in everyday terms → the most natural answer → an objection to it → a better answer → a deeper objection → an honest conclusion that it is still open, with no names, jargon or footnotes.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **The nine questions are the canonical "Philosophy 101" node set.** They match MIT 24.00, Crash Course, Coursera Edinburgh and Cambridge IA almost one-to-one. This is strong evidence for LeetLit's intro philosophy path.
- **The "natural answer → objection → revision" ladder** is a template for an interactive exercise: the learner commits to an answer, gets the strongest objection, and revises.
