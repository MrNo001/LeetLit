# Jostein Gaarder: *Sophie's World*

> The best-selling philosophy book ever: **40+ million copies in 59 languages** (as of 2011), and "the best-selling book in the world" in 1995. A 14-year-old, Sophie, receives anonymous letters from the philosopher Alberto Knox. Each letter is a lesson, and the novel *is* the course.

## How a "lesson" works
1. **Question first:** the first letters contain only questions ("Who are you?" "Where does the world come from?"). Sophie must think *before* being taught.
2. **Lesson letter:** Alberto's explanation of the era or thinker.
3. **Dialogue:** Sophie questions, objects and connects to her life.
4. **Plot advance:** the metafictional mystery (Sophie and Alberto discover they are characters in a book written by Albert Knag for his daughter Hilde) dramatizes Berkeley's idealism and the question of reality.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Question before lesson:** asking the learner to answer "Who are you?" before reading about Socrates is an easy pre-question pattern for every node.
- **Narrative wrapper:** a story arc can carry 35 chronological lessons. It is a possible gamification layer for a beginner path.
- **Era-heavy chapters** (Hellenism, Middle Ages, Renaissance, Baroque, Romanticism) make good cross-discipline hubs with art and literature.
