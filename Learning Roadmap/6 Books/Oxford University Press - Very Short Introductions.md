# Oxford University Press: *Very Short Introductions* (VSI)

> More than 800 pocket-sized primers, launched in 1995. Each is under 200 pages and written by an expert for the general reader, which makes the series the closest thing in the humanities to a standardized **topic explainer**.

## Format
- **Same formula for every title:** a short overview by an expert in the field, with illustrations, a further-reading list and an index. Many titles have been revised in second editions, and some have been replaced outright: Stanley Wells's *William Shakespeare* (2015) replaced Germaine Greer's 2002 volume.
- **Online:** institutions can subscribe to an online service so their users can read the books (via Oxford Academic).

## How a title works
- A few short chapters frame the topic, explain its key ideas or works, and lay out its debates, then point to further reading. There is no fixed reading order across titles.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **A standard primer unit:** VSI shows that one fixed format (same length, same structure, an expert author) can span 800+ topics. LeetLit's topic intros could follow a single template so every node feels consistent.
- **Three kinds of topic:** the series mixes fields (*Ethics*), thinkers (*Kant*) and periods or movements (*Renaissance Art*). That is a ready vocabulary for node types: problem area, person, period.
- **Further reading as outgoing edges:** each title ends by pointing to the next books, the same role as "next" links in a roadmap.
