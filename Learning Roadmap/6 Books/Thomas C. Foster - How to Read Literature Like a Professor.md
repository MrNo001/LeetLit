# Thomas C. Foster: *How to Read Literature Like a Professor*

> The most **NeetCode-like** book in literature: a catalog of **recurring patterns** that professors spot automatically (quests, meals, weather, illness, baptism…), each taught in a short chapter with examples and then applied to an unseen story at the end.

## The pattern catalog (chapters of the original 2003 edition)
| # | Pattern (chapter) | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every Trip Is a Quest (Except When It's Not) | Quester, destination, stated reason, challenges, *real* reason (self-knowledge) |
| 2 | Nice to Eat with You: Acts of Communion | Shared meals = community or its failure |
| 3 | Nice to Eat You: Acts of Vampires | Exploitation, predation, corruption of innocence |
| 4 | If It's Square, It's a Sonnet | Form as meaning |
| 5 | Now, Where Have I Seen Her Before? | Intertextuality: all stories talk to other stories |
| 6 | When in Doubt, It's from Shakespeare… | Shakespearean allusion |
| 7 | …Or the Bible | Biblical allusion |
| 8 | Hanseldee and Greteldum | Fairy-tale structures |
| 9 | It's Greek to Me | Myth |
| 10 | It's More Than Just Rain or Snow | Weather as symbol |
| 11 | …More Than It's Gonna Hurt You: Concerning Violence | Violence as meaning |
| 12 | Is That a Symbol? | How symbols work (open, not one-to-one) |
| 13 | It's All Political | Social and political subtext |
| 14 | Yes, She's a Christ Figure, Too | Christ-figure markers |
| 15 | Flights of Fancy | Flight = freedom or escape |
| 16–17 | It's All About Sex… / …Except Sex | Displacement and indirection |
| 18 | If She Comes Up, It's Baptism | Drowning and rebirth |
| 19–20 | Geography Matters… / …So Does Season | Place and the seasonal cycle |
| 21 | Marked for Greatness | Physical marks and scars |
| 22 | He's Blind for a Reason, You Know | Blindness and insight |
| 23–24 | It's Never Just Heart Disease… / …And Rarely Just Illness | Illness as metaphor |
| 25 | Don't Read with Your Eyes | Read from the work's own historical vantage |
| 26 | Is He Serious? And Other Ironies | Irony overrides everything |
| 27 | A Test Case | Katherine Mansfield's "The Garden Party," applied with the patterns above |

Interludes: "Does He Mean That?" (authorial intention) and "One Story" (all literature as one big story). The 2014 revised edition adds new chapters, a new preface and epilogue, and updated examples, so chapter numbering differs.

## Chapter anatomy
- Name the pattern → 3–6 examples across canonical and popular works → caveats ("except when it's not") → how to use it as a reader.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Foster's chapters can serve almost directly as LeetLit's literature pattern tags** (Quest, Communion, Weather, Baptism, Christ Figure, Illness, Irony…). Each text node can be tagged, and pattern-drill sets can pull short passages from many texts, as NeetCode groups problems by technique.
- **"Test case" at the end:** pattern training followed by an unseen full text is a ready assessment design.
- **Patterns need caveats:** the book's refrain ("except when it's not") argues for exercises that reward *justified* pattern use, not reflexive symbol-hunting.
