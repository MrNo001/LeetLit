# Mortimer Adler and Charles Van Doren: *How to Read a Book*

> The operating manual of the Great Books movement. It treats reading as a **trainable skill with explicit levels and rules**, which makes it the closest existing thing to a "patterns and techniques" guide for humanities texts.

## The four levels of reading (cumulative)
| Level | Name | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Elementary** | Decode the sentences: what does the sentence say? |
| 2 | **Inspectional** | Systematic skimming (title, contents, index, key chapters) and superficial reading (read straight through without stopping). Answers *what kind of book is this and what is it about?* in limited time |
| 3 | **Analytical** | Thorough reading by rules, in three stages (below) |
| 4 | **Syntopical** | Read *many* books on one subject: find relevant passages, bring the authors to common terms, frame the questions, define the issues, analyze the discussion |

### Analytical reading: three stages (15 rules)
- **Structural (what is it about?):** classify the book · state its unity in a sentence or two · outline its major parts · define the problem(s) the author tries to solve
- **Interpretive (what exactly does it say?):** come to terms with key words · grasp the leading propositions · know the arguments · determine which problems were solved
- **Critical (is it true? so what?):** don't judge before you understand · disagree reasonably, not contentiously · distinguish knowledge from opinion. Criticize only by showing the author is *uninformed, misinformed, illogical* or *incomplete*: Adler holds that "one may not disagree with an argument unless one can find fault in its reasoning, facts, or premises."

### Part 3: reading by genre
Separate guidance for practical books, imaginative literature (stories, plays, poems), history, science and mathematics, philosophy, and social science.

## The reading list (Appendix A)
**137 numbered entries, roughly chronological, from Homer to Solzhenitsyn** (1972 edition). It is the canonical "great books" list for self-learners.

## Exercises (Appendix B)
"Exercises and Tests at the Four Levels of Reading": graded practice items that check comprehension and analytical reading of selected passages.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **The four levels map to a difficulty ladder:** inspectional = Easy (what kind of text, what's it about), analytical = Medium (structure, terms, propositions, arguments), syntopical = Hard (compare 3+ authors on one question).
- **The 15 analytical rules can become a reusable exercise template:** "state the unity in one sentence," "outline the parts," "identify the key term," "is the author uninformed, misinformed, illogical or incomplete?"
- **Syntopical reading is the humanities "system design" problem,** the capstone skill after single-text mastery.
