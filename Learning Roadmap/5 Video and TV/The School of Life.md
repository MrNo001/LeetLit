# The School of Life

> **Philosophy as consolation:** short animated essays that reduce a thinker (Epicurus, Montaigne, Nietzsche, Proust, Hokusai…) to one idea that is useful for living. It is a massive reach channel and a cautionary example of oversimplification.

## The path
- **Curriculum-style playlists** by discipline: Western philosophy (Socrates → Sartre), Eastern philosophy, political theory, sociology, literature (Tolstoy, Austen, Proust…), art and architecture, plus psychotherapy.
- Inside a playlist, videos are loosely chronological. There is no progression in difficulty and each video stands alone.
- Since 2016 there has been a large book line (98 titles as of 2025) that re-packages the same ideas.

## Video anatomy (~5–10 min)
1. A life problem (anxiety, envy, love).
2. "The thinker who can help": a quick biography.
3. One central idea, rendered as a lesson for living.
4. Animated metaphors and a calm narrator.
5. A practical, consoling takeaway.

## Critiques (relevant to LeetLit's credibility)
- A Lao Tzu video was criticized by Prof. Hans-Georg Moeller for **fabricated quotes** and misrepresenting the *Tao Te Ching*.
- Reviewers (*LA Review of Books*, *The New Republic*) have attacked the book line as jargon-laden or thin.
- One philosophy blogger praised its use of philosophy for emotional intelligence.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **"Why should I care?" framing** is what drives the reach. A LeetLit node could open with the life question the idea answers.
- **Accuracy guardrail:** popular simplification needs citations to primary text (chapter and section) on every claim and quote. Otherwise the content repeats the School of Life's credibility problem.
