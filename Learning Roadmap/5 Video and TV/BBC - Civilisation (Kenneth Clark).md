# BBC: *Civilisation* (Kenneth Clark, 1969)

> The founding "authored documentary" of art history on TV. Thirteen episodes trace Western European civilization from the end of the Dark Ages to the early 20th century, **through its art and buildings**, filmed in front of them.

## The path (13 episodes)
| # | Title | Era / focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Skin of Our Teeth | The Dark Ages: Ravenna, Iona, Skellig Michael, the Vikings, Charlemagne's Aachen |
| 2 | The Great Thaw | The 12th-century revival: Cluny, Vézelay, Saint-Denis, Chartres; Abbot Suger |
| 3 | Romance and Reality | Late medieval France and Italy: courtly love, St Francis, Giotto, Dante |
| 4 | Man: the Measure of All Things | Early Renaissance: Florence, Urbino, Mantua; Donatello, Alberti, perspective, van Eyck, Botticelli |
| 5 | The Hero as Artist | Papal Rome: Sistine Chapel, Raphael Rooms; Michelangelo, Raphael, Leonardo |
| 6 | Protest and Communication | The Reformation: Erasmus, Dürer, Luther, Holbein, Montaigne, Shakespeare |
| 7 | Grandeur and Obedience | Counter-Reformation and Baroque: St Peter's, Bernini (*Ecstasy of Saint Teresa*) |
| 8 | The Light of Experience | 17th-century science and Dutch painting: Rembrandt, Hals, Vermeer, Descartes, the Royal Society, Wren |
| 9 | The Pursuit of Happiness | Music and Rococo: Bach, Handel, Haydn, Mozart, Watteau, Bavarian churches |
| 10 | The Smile of Reason | The Enlightenment: Parisian salons, Voltaire, Chardin, Jefferson's Monticello |
| 11 | The Worship of Nature | Romanticism: Rousseau, Wordsworth, the Alps, Turner, Constable |
| 12 | The Fallacies of Hope | After the French Revolution: Napoleon, Beethoven, Byron, Delacroix, Géricault, Rodin |
| 13 | Heroic Materialism | 19th–20th centuries: industrial England, Brunel, New York skyscrapers, Courbet, Millet, Tolstoy |

## Episode anatomy
- Clark speaks to camera **standing in front of the actual works and buildings,** with a personal, opinionated voice ("A Personal View").
- Art is used as evidence of a civilization's values; ideas, music and literature interleave.

## Reception and successor
- Influential and much criticized: a "great man approach" with little economics or politics, a focus on male artists (Mary Beard among the critics), and Western Europe only. Clark himself admitted he could not find a way to make law and philosophy visually interesting.
- **Civilisations (BBC/PBS, 2018):** a 9-part successor presented by Simon Schama, Mary Beard and David Olusoga, covering Graeco-Roman and non-European cultures.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Cross-discipline era nodes:** each episode fuses art, literature, music and philosophy of one period, as Yale DS does. This supports "era hubs" that link the three LeetLit disciplines.
- **The personal, opinionated voice** makes it memorable. LeetLit could allow curated "personal view" paths by named experts alongside neutral core paths.
