# BBC: *The Great Philosophers* (Bryan Magee, 1987)

> Fifteen studio conversations in which Bryan Magee asks one of the world's leading scholars to explain one great philosopher. It is a still-unmatched model of **the expert dialogue as lecture**. It followed Magee's *Men of Ideas* (1978), which did the same for contemporary philosophy.

## Episode anatomy
1. Magee frames the philosopher's place in history and why they matter now.
2. A **question-led walkthrough** of the core system. Magee asks the "naive" questions a learner would.
3. The expert explains, Magee paraphrases back ("So what you're saying is…"), and the expert corrects or refines.
4. Assessment: what survives, what fails, and the legacy.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Paraphrase-and-correct** is a powerful pedagogical loop. An AI tutor in LeetLit could make the learner paraphrase and then correct them, in the Magee role reversed.
- **The 15-node canon** (Plato → Wittgenstein) is a compact "core history" list that recurs almost exactly in Durant, Russell, Warburton and the Great Courses.
