# BBC: *Ways of Seeing* (John Berger, 1972)

> A direct, Marxist-inflected reply to Clark's *Civilisation*. It teaches that **"seeing comes before words"** and that how we look at images is shaped by reproduction, gender, property and advertising. It is short (4 episodes, 7 essays) and has been assigned in intro courses for 50 years.

## The path
| Episode / essay | Argument |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Reproduction:** the camera changes the meaning of paintings by detaching them from place (after Walter Benjamin) |
| 2 | **The female nude:** "men act and women appear"; the "ideal spectator" is assumed male |
| 3 | **Oil painting as property:** oil paintings celebrate and display possession |
| 4 | **Publicity (advertising):** a continuation of oil painting's language in consumer capitalism |
| Book | 7 essays, 4 with words and 3 made only of images that the reader must "read" |

## Format notes
- Berger addresses the camera directly and invites skepticism *of himself* ("I hope you will consider what I arrange, but be skeptical of it").
- The pictorial essays have no text at all: the reader works out the argument from juxtaposed images.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **The "picture-only essay" is an exercise type.** Show a sequence of images and ask the learner to articulate the argument it makes.
- **Critical lenses for art** (economic, gender, reproduction and media) parallel the literary-theory lenses in Paul Fry's course. Advanced art nodes should include a lens layer.
- **Short and canonical:** a four-part arc that is easy to assign as an "intermediate theory" path.
