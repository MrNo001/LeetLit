# BBC: *The Shock of the New* (Robert Hughes, 1980)

> The canonical popular history of **modern art**, from Impressionism to the late 20th century. It is organized by **themes** that cut across movements, which makes it the modern-art complement to *Civilisation*.

## The path (8 episodes)
| # | Title | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Mechanical Paradise | How technology shaped art from 1880 to the end of WWI: Cubism, Futurism |
| 2 | The Powers That Be | Modern art and political power: Dada, Constructivism, state architecture |
| 3 | The Landscape of Pleasure | Nature and visions of paradise, 1870s–1950s: Impressionism, Fauvism |
| 4 | Trouble in Utopia | The ideals and failures of modern architecture and urban planning |
| 5 | The Threshold of Liberty | Surrealism: art free from restriction, the unconscious |
| 6 | The View from the Edge | Artists drawing on inner worlds: Expressionism and its heirs |
| 7 | Culture as Nature | Art about the man-made world: Pop art, celebrity, mass media |
| 8 | The Future That Was | Commercialization of art, the decline of modernism, land, performance and body art |

## Episode anatomy
- A critic-presenter with strong opinions, filmed at the works, cutting between artworks and archival footage. Each theme is traced across decades and movements.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Theme-over-chronology for modernism:** movements overlap so much in 1880–1980 that a thematic path (machine, power, pleasure, utopia, unconscious, spirit, mass culture) may teach better than a timeline. It is a candidate "modern art" path.
- **Critical voice:** like Clark and Berger, Hughes argues *for* positions. LeetLit's art content should present interpretations as arguments with evidence, not neutral facts.
