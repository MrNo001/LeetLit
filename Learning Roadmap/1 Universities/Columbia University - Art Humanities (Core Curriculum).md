# Columbia University: Art Humanities ("Art Hum")

> Teaches students "how to look at, think about, and critically discuss the visual arts." It is "not a historical survey, but an analytical study of a limited number of monuments and artists."

## At a glance
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Course | HUMA UN1121, Masterpieces of Western Art (one semester, part of the Core since 1947) |
| Class size | Capped at 22, discussion-based |
| Signature method | Close visual analysis, ideally in front of the real object. New York City's museums, buildings and monuments serve as the lab |
| Variation | 30+ sections per semester, each with its own weekly syllabus built on a shared master syllabus |

**Earlier 12-unit version** (older master syllabus): Parthenon, Amiens Cathedral, Raphael, Michelangelo, Bruegel, Bernini, Rembrandt, Goya, Monet, Picasso, Wright / Le Corbusier, Pollock / Warhol. The revision kept the "few monuments, deep" method and paired canonical men with women and artists of color, such as Anguissola, Roldán, Peeters, Kauffman, Morisot, Cassatt, Bearden, Basquiat and Sherman.

The master syllabus gives each unit a biography, historical background, key ideas, and recommended readings.

## How a session works
- Discussion in front of images, with the instructor prompting students to describe before interpreting: form, material, composition, then context and meaning.
- Field trips to the Met, MoMA, the Cloisters and city architecture turn "looking" into a practiced skill rather than slide memorization.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Depth over coverage:** about 10 units in a whole semester. For art, the "problem" unit is a single object studied slowly.
- **Pairing as a curriculum move:** units built as comparisons (Monet / Manet / Morisot / Cassatt) generate natural compare-and-contrast exercises.
- **Observation first, then interpretation.** This ordering could be enforced in LeetLit's art exercises, for example "describe 5 formal features before reading the context card."
