# University of Oxford: Philosophy in PPE (and the Oxford tutorial model)

> The Oxford model is the **tutorial**: every week you read a list, write an essay, and defend it to a tutor with one or two other students. Philosophy in year 1 is three strands (**General Philosophy, Moral Philosophy, Logic**), followed by a large menu of specialist papers.

## At a glance
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Typical week | 6–8 lectures; "typically two tutorials or one tutorial and one class"; private study is spent mostly on essays. Student accounts report up to 3 tutorials and 2 essays a week |
| Tutorial size | Pairs or triples |
| Year-1 exam | Preliminary Examination, three written papers (Philosophy, Politics, Economics) sat at the end of the third term |
| Weight | Finals (end of year 3) determine the degree. Tutorials are formative and give constant feedback |

## The path

### Year 1: "Introduction to Philosophy" (Prelims)
| Term | Strand | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Michaelmas (T1) | **General Philosophy** (tutorials) | "the foundations of knowledge, scepticism, the nature of the mind and its relation to the body, personal identity, and free will." Classic texts (e.g. Descartes) are read with contemporary critical responses |
| Michaelmas (T1) | **Introductory Logic** (college classes) | "patterns of valid inference" and formal systems, with exercises and proofs. Textbook: Volker Halbach, *The Logic Manual* (written for the course) |
| Hilary (T2) | **Moral Philosophy** (tutorials) | Studied "in connection with J. S. Mill's *Utilitarianism*": happiness and pleasure, the criterion of right action, moral principles, justice |
| Trinity (T3) | Revision, then Prelims | Three written papers |

**Pre-arrival reading (Somerville College, 2025):** Descartes, *Meditations* 1–2; Mill, *Utilitarianism* ch. 1–2; Plato, *Republic* Book 1; Philippa Foot, "Morality as a System of Hypothetical Imperatives" (1972). Suggested primers: Simon Blackburn, *Think*; Conee & Sider, *Riddles of Existence*.

### Years 2–3: Final Honour School (menu of papers)
Philosophy papers are numbered (101, 102, …) and shared across all Oxford philosophy degrees. Confirmed from current lecture lists:
- **101 Early Modern Philosophy** (Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz, Locke, Berkeley, Hume)
- **102 Knowledge and Reality** (contemporary epistemology and metaphysics)
- **103 Ethics** (normative ethics, metaethics, applied ethics)
- **104 Philosophy of Mind** (mind–body, mental content, perception)
- **107 Philosophy of Religion** (normally presumes 101 or 102)
- **115 Plato: *Republic***

Other long-standing Finals options include Aristotle's *Nicomachean Ethics*, Philosophy of Logic and Language, Aesthetics, Medieval Philosophy, Kant, Post-Kantian Philosophy, Frege, Russell and Wittgenstein, Later Wittgenstein, Philosophy of Science and of Physics, Philosophy of Mathematics, Philosophy of Cognitive Science, Philosophical Logic, Practical Ethics, and a thesis option. See the Examination Regulations for the authoritative current list.

## How a tutorial works
1. The tutor sets a question and a reading list (a classic text plus 4–8 articles).
2. The student writes a 1,500–2,500-word essay that takes a position. The length is typical practice, not regulated.
3. In the tutorial (~1 hr) the essay is read or pre-submitted, and the tutor presses objections. The student must defend, concede, or revise.
4. The next week brings a new topic and a new essay. Over a term that is about 8 essays per paper.
- Learning goal (Somerville): "how to read and to evaluate philosophical writings, how to identify the author's arguments and conclusions, and how to think critically and write lucidly."

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **The "one question → one essay → one defense" loop** is the humanities version of "one problem → one solution → code review." LeetLit could reproduce the loop with written answers and objection-pressing feedback (human, peer, or AI).
- **Year-1 triad (epistemology/metaphysics + ethics + logic)** is the standard on-ramp across Oxford, Cambridge and AQA. It is a strong default for the first stretch of a philosophy roadmap.
- **Logic as a separate drill track,** taught through exercises rather than essays, maps directly to auto-graded practice.
