# MIT OpenCourseWare: 24.00 *Problems of Philosophy*

> MIT's free intro course is the archetype of the **problems-first** introduction. It deliberately avoids a chronological survey, and its problem menu has changed with each instructor.

## Versions and their problem sets
| Version | Instructor | Problems covered (per course description) |
|---|---|---|
| Fall 2005 | Sally Haslanger | Existence of God · reason and faith · personal identity and immortality · free will · moral responsibility · standards for moral conduct |
| Fall 2010 | Richard Holton | Knowledge vs opinion · objectivity of moral judgment · paradoxes · mind–body relations · free will · personal identity over time |
| Fall 2019 | Miriam Schoenfield | "takes a systematic rather than historical approach": ethics, metaphysics, theory of knowledge, and philosophy of logic, language and science. Four papers; lecture handouts published |

**The stable core** across versions: **knowledge and skepticism → mind/body → free will → personal identity → ethics (moral objectivity, right action)**, with God, paradoxes and language as rotating extras. It matches Crash Course Philosophy, Coursera Edinburgh, Nagel's *What Does It All Mean?* and Cambridge Part IA almost exactly.

## Course anatomy (OCW package)
- Syllabus → calendar (topic per session) → readings (classic plus contemporary articles) → lecture handouts or notes → paper prompts.
- OCW publishes the *materials*, not usually videos. It is a self-study kit rather than a watch-along course.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- The problems-based intro has a **near-canonical problem list** that LeetLit can adopt as its "Philosophy 101" path without controversy.
- OCW's packaging (syllabus, calendar, readings, handouts, prompts) is a good minimum content schema for any LeetLit module.
