# University of Oxford: English Language and Literature

> A three-year **period survey of all literature in English** (650 to the present), driven by weekly tutorial essays. The period papers have **no set texts**, so students build their own portfolio of authors inside each date band.

## The path
### Year 1 (Prelims, 4 papers)
| Paper | Content | Typical teaching (college) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction to English Language and Literature | Skills paper: approaches to reading, literary theory, and the English language (social, historical, rhetorical use) | 16 compulsory faculty lectures + ~8 college classes + ~4 tutorials. **Assessed by portfolio:** 2 pieces of 1,500–2,000 words, one language commentary on a self-chosen extract and one literature essay |
| 2. Early Medieval Literature, c. 650–1350 | Old English and early Middle English, with commentary on set passages | ~6 tutorials + ~12 classes |
| 3. Literature in English, 1830–1910 | Victorian period | ~6 tutorials + ~4 classes |
| 4. Literature in English, 1910–present | Modern and contemporary | ~6 tutorials + ~4 classes |

### Years 2–3 (Final Honour School, Course I)
| Paper | Content |
|---|---|
| Literature in English 1350–1550 | Chaucer to the early Tudors |
| Literature in English 1550–1660 | Renaissance (Spenser, Donne, Milton, …) |
| Literature in English 1660–1760 | Restoration and Augustan |
| Literature in English 1760–1830 | Romantic |
| Shakespeare | Portfolio of 3 essays on self-chosen topics (some take it in Year 2) |
| Special Options | Faculty seminar, 6,000-word extended essay |
| Dissertation | 8,000 words, own topic |

**Course II** (Medieval Literature and Language) swaps in 650–1550 literature plus the history of English to 1800. All period papers are examined by **three-hour written exams at the end of Year 3**.

## How the teaching works (from the Faculty handbook)
- **Tutorial (typically 2 students):** "focus[es] on written essays and will often allow the student's own writing to set the intellectual agenda. The essay will form the basis for a wide-ranging discussion." Essays are weekly or near-weekly.
- **College class (~8 students):** contextual or textual work and oral analysis.
- **Faculty lectures:** "demonstrations of the application of critical method to literary materials." These are optional apart from the Paper 1 core series.
- **Why no set texts:** "The period papers' avoidance of set texts... encourages wide reading, gives the students freedom to negotiate their own portfolio of authors and allows exploration and innovation alongside study of the 'canon'."
- **Formative feedback** comes from tutorials, practice exams ("collections"), and termly tutorial reports.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Date bands as the roadmap backbone** (650–1350, 1350–1550, 1550–1660, 1660–1760, 1760–1830, 1830–1910, 1910–present) is the standard Anglophone literary periodization. It is ready-made for a literature roadmap.
- **"Choose your own texts within a band"** suggests a path model where a node is a *period* with a pool of works, and the learner picks N. This is unlike NeetCode's fixed problem list.
- **Skills paper first** (methods, theory and language before the periods): a "how to read" module should precede content paths.
