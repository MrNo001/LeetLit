# University of Cambridge: Philosophy Tripos

> The clearest **"core → options → specialization" funnel** of any source here: "As the course proceeds the number of optional elements increases, so that in Part II there are no compulsory subjects." It is analytic in emphasis: "special emphasis on precise and careful argument."

## The path

### Part IA (Year 1): all papers compulsory, no prior philosophy assumed
| Paper | Topics (2025–26) |
|---|---|
| 1. Metaphysics | Causation · Free will · Induction and Miracles · Personal Identity · Mind and Matter: Dualism and Functionalism |
| 2. Ethics and Political Philosophy | Metaethics (fact/value, objectivity) · Normative ethics (consequentialism, deontology, virtue ethics) · Moral psychology (egoism/altruism, empathy) · Political obligation (social contract, fair play, anarchism) · Equality of opportunity |
| 3. Meaning | Conditionals · Frege and Russell on names and descriptions · Necessity, analyticity, the a priori · Grice and Lewis on intention and convention · Causal theories of names · Verificationism |
| 4. Set Texts | **Plato, *Meno* · Descartes, *Meditations* · J. S. Mill, *On Liberty* and *The Subjection of Women*** |
| 5. Formal Methods | Object/metalanguage, use/mention, validity · truth-functional logic · first-order logic · consistency, soundness, completeness · sets and relations · elementary probability. *No supervisions: learned through lectures, logic classes and independent study* |

### Part IB (Year 2): one compulsory paper, the General Paper, and 3 options
- **Compulsory, Knowledge, Language and the World:** nature of knowledge, scepticism, primary/secondary qualities, logical form, truth, modality
- **Options:** History of Analytic Philosophy (Frege, Russell, Wittgenstein's *Tractatus*, Ramsey) · Ethics · Greek and Roman Philosophy (2025–26 set text: Plato's *Phaedo*) · Early Modern (Cavendish, Conway, Leibniz, Hume + themes) · Epistemology and Metaphysics of Science · Political Philosophy (democracy, equality, liberty, property) · Experimental Psychology · Aesthetics and Philosophy of Art · Jurisprudence
- **General Paper:** one philosophical essay chosen from at least 15 questions

### Part II (Year 3): no compulsory subjects, choose 4 papers + General Paper or Dissertation
Metaphysics (realism, universals, time, self including non-Western no-self views) · Philosophy of Mind (consciousness, intentionality) · Ethics (animals, environment, future generations, metaethics, Kantian ethics) · **Kant** (*CPR*, *Groundwork*, *Critique of Judgement*) · Philosophy in the Long Middle Ages (Latin, Arabic and Hebrew traditions, c. 400–1700) · Philosophy of Science · Mathematical Logic (completeness, Gödel) · Philosophical Logic · Wittgenstein and his Successors (*Investigations*, Anscombe, Austin) · Political Philosophy (global justice, feminism, race, Marxism) · borrowed Classics papers on Plato and Aristotle

### Prerequisite graph (as stated in the Guide)
- Part II **Metaphysics** assumes IA Metaphysics.
- Part II **Philosophical Logic** and **Wittgenstein** assume IA Meaning + IA Formal Methods + IB Knowledge, Language and World (History of Analytic Philosophy is desirable).
- Part II **Mathematical Logic** assumes IA Formal Methods + IB KLW, and recommends Further Maths A-level.
- **Kant** and **Philosophy of Mind** build on IA Set Texts and the IB Early Modern paper.
- Every IB topic "uses elementary notions from formal logic," so students without IA Formal Methods are told to work through *forall x: Cambridge*.

## How teaching and assessment work
- **Supervisions:** small groups (1–3 students) with an essay per topic, as at Oxford, but organized by colleges against the faculty syllabus.
- **Exam format:** "answer three questions out of at least ten set." The selection itself is a skill.
- **Coursework:** in IB and II, a pair of extended essays (each ≤4,000 words, on different main topics) can replace one paper, and a Part II dissertation (≤8,000 words) can replace the General Paper. Up to 40% of Part II can be coursework.
- **Anti-recycling rule:** no credit for "using essentially the same content to make essentially the same point" across answers.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **An explicit prerequisite DAG** taken from a real syllabus (IA Meaning + Formal Methods → IB KLW → II Philosophical Logic / Wittgenstein). It can serve as a skeleton for LeetLit's philosophy roadmap edges.
- **Set texts are tiny (3 authors) but deep,** and they pair the problem papers with canonical primary sources.
- **"Choose 3 of 10"** exams reward breadth plus depth. A LeetLit assessment mode could offer a pool of prompts per node and require N answers.
