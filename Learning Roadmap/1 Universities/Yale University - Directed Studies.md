# Yale University: Directed Studies

> Since 1946, a year-long great-books program in three interlocking courses (**Literature, Philosophy, Historical and Political Thought**) taken together by a cohort of first-years. The best-documented example of *parallel synchronized tracks*.

## At a glance
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Structure | Three full-year courses taken together: Literature (DRST 001/002), Philosophy (DRST 003/004), Historical and Political Thought (DRST 005/006) |
| Weekly load | Per track: 1 lecture (50 min) + 2 seminars, so 3 lectures + 6 seminars a week in total |
| Seminars | "The heart and soul of DS." Small groups (max ~15) led by faculty |
| Cohort | ~100–125 students (~10% of first-years), selected by application |
| Extras | Guest colloquia, writing tutors dedicated to DS, prescribed editions so everyone is "on the same page — literally!" |
| AI policy | "Collaboration with ChatGPT or other AI composition software is not permitted" (Lit syllabus, Spring 2025) |

## How the tracks interlock
The three tracks move through the same centuries in the same weeks. In fall, *Republic* shows up in both Philosophy and HPT, Augustine shows up in all three tracks, and the Bible is shared by Literature and HPT. A student meets the same culture from three angles in one week.

## How a session works
- **Lecture (50 min):** a rotating faculty specialist frames the week's text. In Philosophy, eight different lecturers gave the 13 fall lectures.
- **Seminars (2×/week):** faculty-led close discussion of assigned sections, with the same edition for everyone so page references work.
- **Writing:** papers are spaced about every 4–5 weeks per track and staggered across tracks, so something is always due. Weekly discussion posts keep reading honest.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Synchronized tracks:** a "time period" can be the shared key across Literature, Philosophy and Politics/History (and Art). The same week, the same century, three lenses. This makes a strong roadmap layout.
- **Granularity benchmark:** about 13 units per semester per track, each unit a week of reading. Big texts get split into 2–4 numbered chunks (*Iliad* 1–8 / 9–16 / 17–24), which is a ready-made model for "problem sets."
- **Low-stakes weekly writing** (discussion posts) plus periodic graded essays is a proven practice cadence.
