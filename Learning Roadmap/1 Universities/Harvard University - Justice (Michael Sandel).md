# Harvard University: *Justice* (Michael Sandel)

> One of the most-watched philosophy courses ever. Sandel teaches moral and political philosophy by **putting a concrete dilemma to a huge room, polling it, cross-examining volunteers, and only then naming the theory** their intuitions imply.

## The path (24 lectures; the TV series pairs them into 12 episodes)
| # | Lecture | Theory / case |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Moral Side of Murder | Trolley-style dilemmas: kill one to save five? |
| 2 | The Case for Cannibalism | **Bentham's utilitarianism**; *R v Dudley and Stephens* (shipwreck, cabin boy) |
| 3 | Putting a Price Tag on Life | Cost-benefit analysis of human life |
| 4 | How to Measure Pleasure | **J. S. Mill**, higher vs lower pleasures (Shakespeare vs *Fear Factor* vs *The Simpsons*) |
| 5 | Free to Choose | **Libertarianism**: is taxation like forced labor? |
| 6 | Who Owns Me? | Self-ownership, luck, inheritance; student "Team Libertarian" debate |
| 7 | This Land Is Your Land | **Locke**: natural rights, property, state of nature |
| 8 | Consenting Adults | Locke on tacit consent and majority rule |
| 9 | Hired Guns? | Civil War draft substitutes vs volunteer army: markets and fairness |
| 10 | For Sale: Motherhood | Surrogacy, egg/sperm markets, the *Baby M* case |
| 11 | Mind Your Motive | **Kant**: duty and moral worth |
| 12 | The Supreme Principle of Morality | Kant's categorical imperative (spelling-bee example) |
| 13 | A Lesson in Lying | Kant on lying (murderer at the door; misleading truths) |
| 14 | A Deal Is a Deal | **Rawls**: principles chosen behind the veil of ignorance |
| 15 | What's a Fair Start? | Rawls vs meritocracy; arbitrariness of talent and birth order |
| 16 | What Do We Deserve? | Desert and pay (O'Connor vs Judge Judy) |
| 17 | Arguing Affirmative Action | *Hopwood v. Texas* |
| 18 | What's the Purpose? | **Aristotle**: justice as giving people their due, by the *telos* of the good |
| 19 | The Good Citizen | Aristotle on politics and civic virtue (the Casey Martin golf-cart case) |
| 20 | Freedom vs. Fit | Aristotle on roles, and his defense of slavery |
| 21 | The Claims of Community | **Communitarianism**: obligations of membership and solidarity |
| 22 | Where Our Loyalty Lies | Patriotism vs universal human rights |
| 23 | Debating Same-Sex Marriage | Can justice be neutral about the good? |
| 24 | The Good Life | Sandel's case for a politics of the common good |

**Macro-arc:** utilitarianism → libertarianism → Locke → markets and morals → Kant → Rawls → desert and affirmative action → Aristotle → community → neutrality. Each theory is introduced as an answer to the previous one's failure cases.

## Lecture anatomy (repeatable template)
1. **Hook:** a vivid case or dilemma, often from the news or a court case.
2. **Poll:** "How many would…?", answered by show of hands.
3. **Cross-examination:** Sandel calls on volunteers by name, makes them state a principle, and stress-tests it with a variant case. He returns to the same named students later in the lecture, so their positions become recurring characters.
4. **Name the theory:** the principle the room just defended *is* Bentham's, Kant's, or Rawls's.
5. **Objection and cliffhanger:** a new case breaks the theory and sets up the next lecture.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Intuition before theory** reverses the textbook order and works at scale. LeetLit ethics items could open with a dilemma poll and show the community distribution (the "Sandel move") before revealing the theory.
- **Cases as test cases:** each theory is checked against edge cases, much like checking an algorithm against test inputs. "Given principle P, what does it say about case X?" makes an auto-checkable exercise type.
- **Cliffhanger sequencing** (each theory introduced through the previous one's counterexample) gives a natural ordering for a theory path.
