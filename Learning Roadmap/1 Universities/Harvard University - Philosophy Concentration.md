# Harvard University: Philosophy Concentration

> This is the shape of a typical **US research-university philosophy major**: distribution across four areas, small-group tutorials, then electives.

## Requirements (basic track)
| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Total | 11 courses (44 credits) |
| Area 1: **Logic** | One course (completed by the end of the first term of senior year, C– or better) |
| Area 2: **Contemporary Metaphysics and Epistemology** | One course |
| Area 3: **Contemporary Ethics / Political Philosophy / Aesthetics** | One course |
| Area 4: **History of Philosophy** | One course (same deadline and grade rule as logic) |
| **Tutorial I (PHIL 97)** | Group tutorial at introductory level on varied topics, typically in spring of sophomore year |
| **Tutorial II (PHIL 98)** | Advanced follow-up tutorial |
| Electives | Five more philosophy courses |

## The implied map of the discipline
**Logic | Metaphysics and Epistemology | Value Theory (ethics, political, aesthetics) | History of Philosophy.** The same four-way split appears at Princeton and in the PhilPapers top-level taxonomy (see `7 Reference and Taxonomies/`). It is the de facto standard partition of academic philosophy in the anglophone world.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- A philosophy path can be **validated as "complete"** by the same rule a major uses: at least one node cleared in each of the four areas, plus depth in one.
- **Tutorials as a social layer:** small-group discussion is a requirement, not an extra. This argues for cohorts or study groups in the app.
