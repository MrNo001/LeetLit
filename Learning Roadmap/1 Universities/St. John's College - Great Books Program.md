# St. John's College: The Great Books Program

> The most radical version of the great-books model. Since 1937 every student follows the same all-required, four-year, chronological reading list, discussed in seminar with no textbooks, no majors and no electives in the first two years. Philosophy, literature, mathematics, science and music are read in the original works.

## At a glance
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 1937 (the "New Program") |
| Structure | Year 1 = Greeks, Year 2 = Romans, Middle Ages and Renaissance, Year 3 = 17th–18th c., Year 4 = 19th–20th c. |
| Components | **Seminar** (the core: discussion of the list), **Tutorials** (Language: Greek then French; Mathematics: Euclid to Einstein; Music), **Laboratory** (science from original papers) |
| Electives | Junior and senior years include some elective "preceptorials" |
| Caveat | The College notes that its public list "may not accurately reflect the current reading list due to recent changes, works that are read only on one campus, works that are read in part, and works that are read by some students as junior and senior elective classes." |

## How a session works
- **Seminar:** ~20 students and two tutors around a table, meeting in the evening twice a week. It traditionally opens with a single question posed by a tutor, and the conversation must stay anchored in the text.
- **Tutorials:** smaller classes where students translate Greek and French, prove Euclid's propositions at the board, and analyze scores. The skill-building happens here.
- **No lecture-based teaching.** A weekly formal lecture exists, but it is an event rather than instruction.
- **Assessment by conversation:** the "don rag" is an oral review where tutors discuss the student's progress *in front of the student*. There are annual essays and a senior essay with a public oral examination.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Chronology as the only organizing rule** is coherent at the scale of a 4-year degree, but it front-loads the hardest texts (Plato's *Parmenides* in year 1). An app would need difficulty tagging *inside* a chronological path.
- **Primary works in every discipline,** including science and math. It is the purest form of "read the source, not the summary."
- **Parallel skill tutorials** (language, math, music) alongside the seminar map neatly onto LeetLit "skill drills" next to "reading paths."
