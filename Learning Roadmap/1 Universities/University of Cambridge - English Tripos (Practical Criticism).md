# University of Cambridge: English Tripos and Practical Criticism

> Cambridge is the home of **Practical Criticism**: analyzing a poem or prose passage *without being told who wrote it or when*. The technique descends from I. A. Richards's experiments in the 1920s (*Practical Criticism*, 1929). It is still compulsory in year 1 **and** in the final year. In NeetCode terms this is the closest thing literature has to solving an unseen problem under time pressure.

## The path
| Part | Components |
|---|---|
| **Part IA (Year 1)** | **Practical Criticism and Critical Practice** (compulsory) · **Shakespeare** (compulsory, assessed by a portfolio of essays submitted in Easter Term) · begin two period papers (examined in IB) |
| **Part IB (Year 2)** | **English Literature and its Contexts, 1300–1550** (compulsory) + three further papers from the list (other period papers such as 1500–1700, 1688–1847, 1830–present; early medieval; etc.) · option: replace one exam with a 5,000-word dissertation |
| **Part II (Year 3)** | Compulsory: **Practical Criticism** (again, at a higher level) · **Tragedy** (a cross-period genre paper from the Greeks to the present) · **Dissertation** on a topic of the student's choosing · plus options. Students may submit two dissertations and sit three exams, or one dissertation and four exams |

## What Practical Criticism trains
- **Unseen passages:** the student meets texts stripped of author, date and context, then analyzes diction, syntax, meter and rhythm, imagery, tone, form, and the voice/speaker, and builds an interpretation from the words alone.
- **Comparison:** pairs of passages force discrimination, such as sentiment versus sentimentality or two treatments of the same theme.
- **Critical Practice:** the companion half, on how criticism itself works (theories and methods of reading).
- **Preparatory reading** college lists recommend: I. A. Richards, *Practical Criticism*; Norton anthologies for broad exposure to unseen-ready texts.

## How teaching works
- **Supervisions** (essays discussed in groups of 1–3) are the core weekly unit, organized by colleges against the faculty's paper list.
- **Practical Criticism classes:** groups work through anonymized passages together, then write timed analyses.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **"Unseen passage" is the purest NeetCode-style exercise for literature.** Give a 14–40-line text with metadata hidden, ask for an analysis or targeted questions (meter, figure, speaker, tone), then reveal the author, date and context. Difficulty can be tuned by period, density and form.
- **Spaced recurrence:** Cambridge makes the same skill compulsory in year 1 and year 3. LeetLit could treat close reading as a skill track that recurs at higher difficulty instead of a one-off module.
- **Genre papers (Tragedy) cut across periods.** This is a second axis (genre) beside the period axis.
