# Yale University (Open Yale Courses): ENGL 300 *Introduction to Theory of Literature* (Paul Fry)

> A canonical map of **"the schools"** of literary theory. These are the methods or lenses an advanced reader can apply to any text. Central questions: "what literature is, how it is produced, how it can be understood, and what its purpose is."

**Reading:** David Richter (ed.), *The Critical Tradition* (anthology). Lectures often test theories on a few recurring literary examples.

## Lecture anatomy
- Background and context for the school → explanation of the anthology essay → demonstration on a literary example → how this school answers or attacks the previous one.
- Each school is explicitly framed as a **reaction** to its predecessor, which gives a dialectical chain.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **"Lenses" are reusable patterns.** Each school is an interpretive algorithm that applies to any text. That makes it the literature equivalent of NeetCode's pattern categories (Two Pointers, Sliding Window…). Exercise idea: "read this passage through a formalist vs. a Marxist vs. a psychoanalytic lens."
- **Prerequisite chain:** hermeneutics/formalism → structuralism → deconstruction → historicist, political and identity-based schools. Theory naturally belongs at the *advanced* tier after close reading.
