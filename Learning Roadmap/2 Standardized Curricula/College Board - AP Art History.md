# College Board: AP Art History

> The closest thing the arts have to a **"Blind 75"**: a fixed, numbered list of **250 required works** that every student must know (identification, context, function, form, significance), plus a set of transferable analysis skills tested on works the student has *never seen*.

The representative examples are drawn from the published image set. Check the CED for the complete numbered list. Percentages are the College Board's current multiple-choice weightings, and the 2013 outline used slightly different figures for Units 3 and 4.

## Skills (the "patterns" of art history)
The CED defines art-historical thinking skills, practiced on known and **unknown** works:
1. **Visual analysis:** describe form, materials, techniques and how they create meaning
2. **Contextual analysis:** function, patronage, audience, historical setting (the largest exam share, about 28–32%)
3. **Comparison of works of art**
4. **Artistic traditions:** how works relate to, continue or break with traditions (about 20–25%)
5. **Visual analysis of unknown works**
6. **Attribution of unknown works:** infer artist, culture or period from style
7. **Art-historical interpretations:** evaluate scholarly claims
8. **Argumentation:** build a claim with visual and contextual evidence

## Exam anatomy
- **Section I:** 80 multiple-choice questions, many in image-based sets.
- **Section II:** 6 free-response questions. Two long essays (a comparison, and a visual/contextual analysis) and four short essays (visual analysis, contextual analysis, **attribution of an unknown work**, and continuity/change across cultures).

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **A numbered canon of 250 is an exact template for LeetLit's art list.** Each work becomes a "problem card" (image, metadata, function, context, form, significance), grouped by region and era, with weights.
- **Attribution of unknowns** is the art counterpart of an unseen coding problem: "Here is an image you haven't studied. Attribute it and justify." This tests transferable pattern recognition, not memory.
- **Global by design:** Units 5–9 (Indigenous Americas, Africa, West and Central Asia, South/East/Southeast Asia, the Pacific) hold 71 of the 250 works (~28%), and Units 1 and 10 are global too. That is a useful counterweight to the Eurocentric popular sources (Gombrich, Clark).
