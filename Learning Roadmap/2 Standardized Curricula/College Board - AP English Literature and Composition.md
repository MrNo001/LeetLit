# College Board: AP English Literature and Composition

> AP Lit has **no required reading list**. It is organized entirely around **skills**, which makes it the most NeetCode-like literature framework: a small set of reusable analytical patterns practiced on ever-harder texts in a spiral.

## The path (9 units, three genre spirals)
| Unit | Genre | Pacing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short Fiction I | ~10 class periods |
| 2 | Poetry I | ~10 |
| 3 | Longer Fiction or Drama I | ~17 |
| 4 | Short Fiction II | ~17 |
| 5 | Poetry II | ~17 |
| 6 | Longer Fiction or Drama II | ~17 |
| 7 | Short Fiction III | ~17 |
| 8 | Poetry III | ~17 |
| 9 | Longer Fiction or Drama III | ~17 |

**Exam weighting by genre:** Short Fiction 42–49% · Poetry 36–45% · Longer Fiction/Drama 15–18%.

## Big ideas (the pattern categories)
| Code | Big idea | Enduring understanding (abridged) |
|---|---|---|
| CHR | Character | Characters let readers explore values, beliefs, biases and norms |
| SET | Setting | Setting conveys values, not just time and place |
| STR | Structure | Arrangement and sequencing are choices that shape interpretation |
| NAR | Narration | The narrator's or speaker's perspective controls details and emphasis |
| FIG | Figurative Language | Comparisons and associations shift meaning from literal to figurative |
| LAN | Literary Argumentation | Interpretations are communicated through evidence-backed arguments |

## Skill categories (what students are asked to *do*)
1. Explain the function of **character**
2. Explain the function of **setting**
3. Explain the function of **plot and structure**
4. Explain the function of the **narrator or speaker**
5. Explain the function of **word choice, imagery, and symbols**
6. Explain the function of **comparison** (simile, metaphor, allusion…)
7. **Develop textually substantiated arguments** about interpretations of part or all of a text

Each skill is broken into progressively harder sub-skills (identify → explain → interpret), and the units revisit the same big ideas at higher complexity.

## Exam anatomy
- **Section I:** 55 multiple-choice questions on passages (prose and poetry).
- **Section II:** 3 essays, about 40 minutes each.
  - **Poetry analysis:** an unseen poem.
  - **Prose fiction analysis:** an unseen passage.
  - **Literary argument:** a thematic prompt answered with a work of the student's choice, often with a list of suggested titles "or another work of equal literary merit."

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **The 6 big ideas × 7 skills grid is a ready-made tag taxonomy for literature exercises,** much as NeetCode tags problems by pattern. Example: "Poem #212: tags FIG, NAR; skill 6."
- **The spiral, not the list, is the backbone.** Each pass increases difficulty on the same patterns, which is how NeetCode's Easy → Medium → Hard progression works within a topic.
- **Unseen-text items** (two of three essays) confirm that transfer to new texts is the gold-standard assessment.
