# AQA: A-level Philosophy (7172)

> England's main pre-university philosophy syllabus. It is a compact, **fully specified** problems-plus-texts curriculum, with each topic tied to named passages in named texts. This is the best example of a syllabus already written at "card" granularity.

## The path (4 topics, each 25%)

### 3.1 Epistemology
**Content:** What is knowledge? (tripartite view, Gettier) · Perception as a source of knowledge (direct realism, indirect realism, idealism) · Reason as a source of knowledge (innatism, intuition and deduction) · The limits of knowledge (scepticism, Descartes, reliabilism…)

**Set texts:** Berkeley, *Three Dialogues* · Descartes, *Meditations* 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 · Gettier, "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?" · Hume, *Enquiry* §2, §4 (pt 1) · Leibniz, *New Essays* (Preface, Bk 1) · Locke, *Essay* (selected) · Plato, *Meno* (from 81e) · Russell, *The Problems of Philosophy* ch. 1–2 · Catharine Trotter Cockburn (attrib.), letter in Berkeley's *Theory of Vision Vindicated* · Zagzebski, "What is Knowledge?"

### 3.2 Moral philosophy
**Content:** Normative ethics (utilitarianism, Kantian deontology, Aristotelian virtue ethics) · Applied ethics (stealing, simulated killing, eating animals, telling lies) · Meta-ethics (moral realism, naturalist and non-naturalist; anti-realism: error theory, emotivism, prescriptivism)

**Set texts:** Annas, "Virtue Ethics" · Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* (selections) · Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic* ch. 1, 6 and *Central Questions* · Bentham, *Principles of Morals and Legislation* ch. 1, 4 · Diamond, "Eating Meat and Eating People" · Foot, "Morality as a System of Hypothetical Imperatives" · Hare, *The Language of Morals* · Hume, *Treatise* III.1 · Kant, *Groundwork* ch. 1–2 · Mackie, *Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong* ch. 1 · Mill, *Utilitarianism* ch. 1, 2, 4, 5 · Moore, *Principia Ethica* §6–14 · Smart & Williams, *Utilitarianism: For and Against*

### 3.3 Metaphysics of God
**Content:** The concept and nature of God (attributes, God and time, incoherence arguments) · Arguments for existence (ontological, teleological/design, cosmological) and the problem of evil · Religious language (cognitivism vs non-cognitivism, verificationism, the "University Debate")

**Set texts:** Anselm, *Proslogium* II–IV and Gaunilo · Aquinas, *Summa* I q.2 a.3, q.25 a.3 · Descartes, *Meditations* 3, 5 · Flew, Hare and Mitchell, "Theology and Falsification" · Hick, *Evil and the God of Love* · Hume, *Dialogues* II, V, VIII, IX and *Enquiry* XI · Leibniz, *Monadology* §32–39 · Malcolm on Anselm · Midgley, *Wickedness* · Paley, *Natural Theology* · Plantinga, *God, Freedom and Evil* · Plato, *Euthyphro* · Stump & Kretzmann, "Eternity" · Swinburne, "The Argument from Design"

### 3.4 Metaphysics of mind
**Content:** What is "mind"? · Dualism (substance, property; issues) · Physicalism (philosophical behaviourism, type identity theory, eliminative materialism) · Functionalism

**Set texts:** Avramides, *Other Minds* · Block, "Troubles with Functionalism" · Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind* ch. 1 and "Consciousness and its Place in Nature" · P. S. Churchland, *Brain-wise* · P. M. Churchland, "Eliminative Materialism…" · Descartes, *Meditations* 6 · Hempel, "The Logical Analysis of Psychology" · Jackson, "Epiphenomenal Qualia" · Putnam, "The Nature of Mental States" · Ryle, *The Concept of Mind* · Princess Elisabeth–Descartes correspondence (May 1643) · Smart, "Sensations and Brain Processes"

## Cross-cutting skills (required in every topic)
- Analyse and evaluate arguments, including conceptual analysis.
- Identify premises, conclusions and inferences. Distinguish deduction, induction and abduction.
- Recognise flaws such as circularity, contradiction and question-begging.
- Use the core terms correctly: analytic/synthetic, a priori/a posteriori, necessary/contingent, objective/subjective, tautology, dilemma, paradox, justification.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Already card-sized:** every content item maps to a passage (e.g. Gettier 1963, full paper; Descartes, *Meditation* 6). This could seed a starter "Philosophy 101" problem bank.
- **The terminology list is a natural first "flashcard deck"** (spaced repetition) that underpins all later items.
- **Classic + contemporary pairing on every topic** (Descartes ↔ Chalmers; Mill ↔ Smart & Williams) is the same move Oxford tutorials make.
