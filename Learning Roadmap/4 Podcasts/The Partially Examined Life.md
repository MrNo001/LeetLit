# The Partially Examined Life (PEL)

> Started in 2009 by "some guys" who once wanted to do philosophy professionally. In each episode the hosts **read a primary text and argue about it**, which recreates a grad-seminar discussion for listeners. There are 400+ numbered episodes.

## The path
- **No sequence:** episodes follow the hosts' reading choices, mixing classics (Plato, Aristotle, Kant, Nietzsche, Heidegger) with contemporary papers and literary works.
- **Navigation is by topic:** the site's "PEL Episodes by Topic" page groups episodes by area (ethics, mind, political, existentialism and others). There is no path, so the listener builds their own.
- **Spin-offs:**
  - **Closereads:** Linsenmayer and Alwan read a text "line by line, stopping frequently" to explain it, in unedited audio and video.
  - **Nightcap** (after-show) and **Not School** (member-led reading groups).

## Episode anatomy
- Intro and context on the author → a host summarizes the text's structure → a free-ranging debate (pushback, tangents, contemporary relevance) → wrap-up. The tone balances "insight and flippancy." Listeners don't need to have read the text, but readers get more out of it.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Seminar-as-content:** listening to smart peers argue about a text you just read is a powerful, cheap learning format. LeetLit could pair each core text with a discussion recording or forum thread.
- **"Close read" mode** (line by line) versus **"seminar" mode** (big-picture debate) are two distinct content types worth offering per text.
- **Topic browsing without a path** works for engaged amateurs but leaves beginners lost. That gap is what LeetLit's roadmap is for.
