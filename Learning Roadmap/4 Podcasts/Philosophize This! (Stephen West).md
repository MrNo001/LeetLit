# Philosophize This! (Stephen West)

> One of the most popular philosophy podcasts. It is a self-taught host explaining the canon in plain language, **one thinker at a time**, starting at the very beginning. By 2026 it has ~245 episodes.

## The path
1. **Chronological foundation (early episodes):** Presocratics → Socrates → Plato → Aristotle → Hellenistic schools (Epicureans, Stoics, Skeptics) → late antiquity and medieval (Augustine, Boethius, Anselm, Aquinas and others) → early modern (Descartes, Hobbes, Spinoza, Locke, Leibniz, Berkeley, Hume) → Enlightenment and Kant → 19th century (Hegel, Schopenhauer, Kierkegaard, Marx, Mill, Nietzsche).
2. **Thematic series (later episodes):** multi-part arcs on 20th–21st-century thought (existentialism, philosophy of mind, critical theory, Foucault, Derrida, Baudrillard, Byung-Chul Han, Charles Taylor, Alasdair MacIntyre…).
3. **Literature as philosophy (recent):** episodes on novels and plays as philosophical texts. Episodes #241–243 are Shakespeare's *Julius Caesar*, *Romeo and Juliet* and *Hamlet*, #238 is *Frankenstein*, and #234 is Kundera's *Unbearable Lightness of Being*.

Recent sample (#226–245): Camus (*The Rebel*, exile, Kafka and *The Fall*) · Ernst Bloch · Kafka and totalitarianism (Arendt, Adorno) · late Wittgenstein · Byung-Chul Han · Dostoevsky and Bataille · Marcus Aurelius and critics of Stoicism · Charles Taylor · Shakespeare · MacIntyre's *After Virtue*.

## Episode anatomy
- **Hook:** an everyday question or puzzle.
- **Context:** who the thinker was responding to, which is often a callback to earlier episodes.
- **Core idea:** walked through slowly, with analogies and repetition.
- **Stakes:** why it matters now. Episodes often close by teeing up the next one.
- The design assumes listeners go in order. The cumulative callbacks reward sequential listening.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Callbacks build a dependency graph implicitly.** Each episode says "remember when Plato…". LeetLit can make those prerequisite links explicit.
- **The beginner on-ramp is chronological** in the most popular solo podcasts (this one and HoPWaG). The problems-first approach dominates university intros instead. LeetLit may want both entry points.
- **Philosophy ↔ literature crossover** (Shakespeare, Kundera, Dostoevsky) shows demand for linked paths between the two disciplines.
