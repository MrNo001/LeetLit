# Literature and History (Doug Metzger)

> A chronological journey through **world literature from 3100 BCE onward**, one major work per long episode, with ~127 episodes so far. It is the literature counterpart of *History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*.

## The path (seasons in order)
Sumerian and Akkadian literature (Mesopotamia; Assyriology) → Ancient Egypt → the Hebrew Bible / Old Testament → Ancient Greece → Rome → the birth of Christianity → Late Antiquity → the dawn of the Middle Ages → **current season: early Islamic history** (as of 2026).

"Episodes can be heard in any order, though most listeners go from the beginning."

## Episode anatomy (~2 hours)
1. **General introduction** to the work: what it is, when it was written and why it matters.
2. **Full summary that assumes no prior knowledge,** narrated and soundtracked with original music.
3. **Analysis** of the cultural, biographical and historical forces behind the work.
- **Supplements:** free, illustrated, footnoted **transcripts** for every episode; **quizzes** for review; 60+ hours of paid bonus content.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **"Summary, then analysis"** is the popular-literature lecture pattern (Crash Course does the same at 10 minutes). LeetLit literature nodes need both a plot layer and an interpretation layer, and should label them clearly.
- **Quizzes and transcripts on a podcast** show the audience wants retrieval practice even from entertainment-style content.
- **Chronological world-literature backbone** (Mesopotamia → Egypt → Hebrew Bible → Greece → Rome …) mirrors Lit Hum and DS fall semesters, which gives strong cross-source agreement on the ancient start of a literature path.
