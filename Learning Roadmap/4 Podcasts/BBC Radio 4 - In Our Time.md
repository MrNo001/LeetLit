# BBC Radio 4: *In Our Time*

> The largest audio archive of expert humanities discussion: **1,089 episodes as of July 2025** since 15 October 1998. Each has a host and "three absolutely top-class academics" on one subject, live, for about 42 minutes. Melvyn Bragg hosted until July 2025, and Misha Glenny took over from January 2026.

## The path
- **No path. It is a vast categorized archive.** The philosophy and culture categories cover most canonical philosophers, literary works (from *Beowulf* to *Middlemarch*), and art movements and artists.
- In effect it works as a **reference shelf**: for nearly any node on a humanities roadmap, there is probably an episode.

## Episode anatomy
1. **Framing:** the host introduces the topic and three guests from different universities.
2. **Chronological walk:** the host "usually moves through the topic chronologically," handing each phase to the relevant specialist.
3. **Expert handoffs:** guests build on and occasionally correct each other.
4. **Synthesis:** the host closes or asks one guest to sum up legacy and significance.
5. **Reading list:** each episode page lists recommended books.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Three specialists per topic** is a good model for "multiple perspectives" content: biography, ideas and reception from different experts.
- **A per-episode reading list** turns each discussion into a gateway to primary and secondary reading. That fits LeetLit's "resources" panel per node.
- **Linkable archive:** LeetLit nodes could link out to relevant *In Our Time* episodes as optional enrichment (free BBC content).
