# Coursera: *Introduction to Philosophy* (University of Edinburgh)

> One of the largest philosophy MOOCs (**~747,000 learners, 4.7★ from ~9,700 reviews**). It is a tour of questions, and **every module title is a question**, taught by a different Edinburgh philosopher.

## The path (14 modules)
| # | Module | Instructor | Topic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is Philosophy? | Dave Ward | Aims and methods of philosophy |
| 2 | Morality: Objective, Relative or Emotive? | Matthew Chrisman | Metaethics: objectivism, relativism, emotivism |
| 3 | What is Knowledge? And Do We Have Any? | Duncan Pritchard | Classical account, Gettier, radical scepticism |
| 4 | *Week 2 Review* | n/a | Quiz |
| 5 | Do We Have an Obligation to Obey the Law? | Guy Fletcher | Political obligation: gratitude, consent, fairness |
| 6 | Should You Believe What You Hear? | Allan Hazlett | Testimony: Hume vs Reid, intellectual autonomy |
| 7 | *Week 3 Review* | n/a | Quiz |
| 8 | Minds, Brains and Computers | Suilin Lavelle | Dualism, physicalism, functionalism, Turing Test, Chinese Room |
| 9 | Are Scientific Theories True? | Michela Massimi | Realism vs antirealism, the "no miracles" argument, constructive empiricism |
| 10 | *Week 4 Review* | n/a | Quiz |
| 11 | Do We Have Free Will and Does It Matter? | Elinor Mason | Determinism, libertarianism, compatibilism, hard determinism |
| 12 | Time Travel and Philosophy | Alasdair Richmond | Grandfather paradox, causal loops |
| 13 | *Week 5 Review* | n/a | Quiz |
| 14 | Peer Review | n/a | Survey + peer-reviewed assignment |

Estimated time ~18 hours (Coursera says "about 2 weeks at 10 hours a week").

## Module anatomy
- **4–7 short videos** (total 25–57 min) with a lecturer explaining a position, then objections.
- **2–3 short readings** (~10 min each).
- **One quiz** (12–30 min), plus a **review module** every two topics (spaced retrieval).

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **Question-as-title** ("Do we have free will and does it matter?") is a strong node-naming convention for a roadmap, because it frames each unit as a problem to solve.
- **Review checkpoints** after every two topics are a cheap form of spaced repetition.
- **Topic set** = the problems-first core again (ethics, knowledge, mind, science, free will), plus engaging "fun" closers (time travel).
