# HarvardX: *Masterpieces of World Literature*

> A **global** great-books course from the editors of the major world-literature anthologies (Puchner: *Norton Anthology of World Literature*; Damrosch: *Longman Anthology of World Literature*). It is built around the idea of how works **circulate beyond their origin**. Notably it is "based not on lectures but on a more vivid dialogue format."

## The path (12 sections, ~12 weeks at 5–7 hrs/week)
| # | Section |
|---|---|
| 1 | Goethe and the Birth of World Literature |
| 2 | *The Epic of Gilgamesh* |
| 3 | Homer, *The Odyssey* |
| 4 | *The Thousand and One Nights* |
| 5 | Murasaki Shikibu, *The Tale of Genji* |
| 6 | Camões, *The Lusiads* |
| 7 | Voltaire, *Candide* |
| 8 | Lu Xun and Eileen Chang |
| 9 | Jorge Luis Borges, *Ficciones* |
| 10 | Wole Soyinka, *Death and the King's Horseman* |
| 11 | Salman Rushdie and Jhumpa Lahiri |
| 12 | Orhan Pamuk, *My Name is Red* |

A shorter spin-off, *Modern Masterpieces of World Literature*, reuses the second half.

## Session anatomy
- **Two-professor dialogue** in place of a monologue lecture. They disagree, pass the argument back and forth, and model critical conversation.
- Context on travel, translation, and how a work "became world literature," followed by close attention to key passages.

## Takeaways for LeetLit
- **The meta-theme starts at node 1:** opening with "what is world literature?" (Goethe's *Weltliteratur*) gives the whole path a question to answer.
- **A non-Western-inclusive canon** of 12 works sets a benchmark for a global literature path, to sit alongside the Columbia and Yale Western spines.
- **Dialogue format** is an alternative "solution video" style: two experts debating a reading.
